# Why do trained worms circle?

Generated by `scripts/why_circling.py`.

## 1. Circling costs almost no extra energy

Hand-coded 0.4 Hz wave with a constant turning bias, 60 s on a flat field. Power is the drag dissipated by the body (the model's energy cost).

| turning bias | power | path speed (mm/s) | net progress (mm/s) | energy per mm of path | energy per mm of net progress |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 0.055 | 0.176 | 0.175 | 0.311 | 0.313 |
| 0.1 | 0.055 | 0.173 | 0.168 | 0.318 | 0.327 |
| 0.2 | 0.055 | 0.165 | 0.150 | 0.336 | 0.368 |
| 0.3 | 0.055 | 0.152 | 0.126 | 0.363 | 0.437 |

Energy per mm *crawled* barely changes with turning; only energy per mm of *net progress* rises, because a circling worm goes nowhere. So an energy cost only punishes circling if getting somewhere matters.
