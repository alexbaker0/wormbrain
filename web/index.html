How the neurons are modelled

Each of the 282 neurons is a single graded unit: like most C. elegans neurons it does not fire spikes, its voltage v just rises and falls smoothly. Every 0.02 s of simulated time, each neuron updates according to

τ dv/dt = − v + gchem · Σ w · release(partner) + ggap · Σ (vpartner − v) + bias + sensory input − adaptation

  • Leak (− v, τ): without input the voltage relaxes back to rest, quickly or slowly depending on the time constant τ (0.05–2 s).
  • Chemical synapses: each partner's release = tanh(gain · v), zero at rest, positive when it releases more transmitter than usual and negative when less. The weight w is that partner's share of the neuron's input synapses in the connectome, signed by the partner's transmitter (Wang et al. 2024): acetylcholine excites, GABA inhibits; glutamate can do either in real worms, so its sign is a model assumption.
  • Gap junctions: electrical connections pull connected neurons' voltages towards each other, in both directions.
  • Adaptation: a slow negative feedback on each neuron's own activity (like a calcium-activated potassium current). It makes responses sag over seconds, and it is what lets the network produce the rhythm that drives crawling.
  • Senses: ASEL responds when salt at the nose rises, ASER when it falls, both relative to the recent average, as found in real worms (Suzuki et al. 2008). The forward motor neurons VB and DB sense how the body just ahead of them is bent (Wen et al. 2012).
  • Muscles and body: each of the 95 body-wall muscles adds up input from the motor neurons that really synapse onto it; dorsal muscles minus ventral ones set how each part of the 16-segment body bends, and the bending pushes against the agar (resistive force theory: sliding lengthwise is much easier than sideways).

Fixed: which neurons connect, how many synapses, and their signs. Learned: 425 numbers: time constant, bias, gain and adaptation strength for each of the 104 neuron classes (left/right and dorsal/ventral partners share them), plus 9 global ones, found by an evolution strategy (CMA-ES) that tried thousands of variants and kept what found food. The real worm's wiring was not tuned at all.
